# Publish Chinese Crypto Writing in English

## Select the editing boundary

Infer the least invasive mode that satisfies the request.

### Faithful

Use when the user asks for translation, verification, proofreading, or says not to change the article.

- Correct mistranslation, grammar, unnatural collocations, and misleading terminology.
- Preserve structure, repetition, examples, claims, and authorial intensity.
- Explain culturally specific metaphors when a direct English equivalent would fail.
- Flag factual claims requiring author confirmation instead of silently rewriting them.

### CT Native

Use when the user explicitly wants English crypto or CT-native expression.

- Apply established crypto terms where they carry the intended meaning.
- Adjust cadence locally and remove obvious translation artifacts.
- Preserve the thesis, evidence, and structure unless the user grants broader freedom.
- Avoid stacking slang. One precise term is better than several performative ones.

### Editorial

Use only when the user requests rewriting, publication editing, restructuring, or a format such as a Substack essay.

- May compress repetition, reorder paragraphs, or strengthen transitions.
- Keep a record of substantive changes.
- Do not replace the author's worldview with that of a named writer.

## Bilingual review

When both source and translation are available, check in this order:

1. Missing, added, or strengthened claims.
2. Logical relationships and section structure.
3. Domain terminology and participant roles.
4. Cultural metaphors and tone.
5. Sentence-level naturalness.

## Deliverables

- Short text: polished text and a short decision note.
- Long document: clean copy plus comments copy by default.
- Comments should distinguish `建议直接改`, `可选 CT 化`, `需要作者确认`, and `需要文化处理` when writing for Chinese-speaking authors.
- Generate tracked changes only when requested or clearly useful to the recipient.
